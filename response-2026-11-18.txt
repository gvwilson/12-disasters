Response to reviewers

Reviewer #1

- "My main concern is the scope of the manuscript and the
  actionability of several tips for its stated audience." I have added
  clarifying notes in several places to distinguish what small teams
  can reasonably be expected to do from what they should ask IT to do
  (if available).

- "Tips 11 and 12 broaden the manuscript from technical disaster prep
  into leadership." I have rewritten the first paragraph of Tip 11 to
  make the shift of focus explicit. (I believe very strongly that the
  human aspects of disaster management are as important as the IT
  aspects, and so have opted to retain these tips.)

- "On the 'Questions for Support': what to do in case there is no such
  support available on site?" Addressed with several comments.

- "Please consider adding web links into in the running text rather
  than having both footnotes and numbered references in case the
  journal allows this." I am using PLOs's own style file here.

- "CRAN is a mirrored network, so there is no single failure point
  once a package is published." Noted, and comment added.

- "Data repositories do not primarily serve as platform for data
  backup, but for data publishing, hence the DOI." Thank you for
  pointing this out - I've added a clarifying note.

- "The note field in Tip 3 seems redundant if the 3-2-1 rule is
  adhered to."  Agreed and addressed.

- "Tip 6 seems to be a rule to monitor tip 1." I've added a backward
  reference from the former to the latter.

- "In case offboarding is suggested, I think mentioning onboarding is
  similarly critical." Agreed - I've added a couple of sentences.

- "Please consider refining Tip 8. This rule is not actionable at all
  for readers subordinate to local IT support." Clarifying note added.

- "If a research software team is small with few members and just
  produces analysis code and some packages, the 12 tips seem
  disproportionate. Please consider adding a small acknowledgement of
  this." With respect, I disagree with the reviewer on this point.

Reviewer #2

- "It feels targeted at a large research software development group
  rather than a typical computational lab." I have added a paragraph
  in "Who You Are" covering the small-lab case (an RSE plus a changing
  cast of students), emphasizing a written record of work, documenting
  where assets live, and a handover checklist, and I have softened the
  large-group urgency in Tip 4.

- "The topic de jour is the safety of AI agents." I have added a
  paragraph in Tip 8 on AI-agent risks (unintended data exfiltration,
  disclosure of confidential information, and local damage from a
  poorly prompted agent). I wish I knew enough to write more, or that
  people were being more deliberate in their adoption of AI tools so
  as not to get ahead of our understanding of the risks...

- "The statement that the US government attacked its own research
  groups is a subjective interpretation of events." Rephrased: "In
  2025, the US government initiated an unprecedented series of cuts to
  its own scientific research groups."

- "Planning for disasters ... will not prevent the disasters, but may
  mitigate their impacts." Changed "prevent them" to "mitigate their
  impacts."

- "I don't understand the assertion that the reader is doing
  everything themselves on top of their regular job." Reframed to note
  that disaster planning is done on top of "normal" work.

- "What is meant by 'done' in this context?" Removed.

- "It seems odd to call out Shiny and Streamlit specifically."
  Generalized to "an interactive web site or web-based application."

- "One doesn't have to release software to a public registry in order
  to be vulnerable." Broadened the profile beyond public registries
  and added the "released package stops working after an OS update and
  its author has left" scenario.

- "This implies that the data has to be deposited in a repository in
  order to be vulnerable." Reworded to "whether or not they are shared
  with collaborators or deposited in repositories."

- "'Our ecology lab' but there is only one author." Changed to "an
  ecology lab" (a notional running example) throughout.

- "What is meant by 'work stops dead'?" Changed to "How long can you
  tolerate being without it before your work is seriously disrupted?"

- "Emphasize that this is an ongoing assessment, not a one-off." Added
  a sentence saying so.

- "Why 30 seconds to find the plan?" Softened to "find quickly."

- "The example will fail if both ... are in an office and there is a
  fire or earthquake." Added "at home" to the desk-drawer example.

- "The reference to 'bus factor' may not be recognizable."  Phrased as
  "lottery-factor (also called a 'bus factor' problem)".

- "There's no role for local backups ... NAS servers that support
  snapshots?"  Added local database snapshots and NAS filesystem
  snapshots.

- "The offsite deposit advice ... doesn't belong with backups."
  Distinguished as "Publishing is a related but distinct step".

- "Does anyone really mirror ... what is a 'secondary remote'?"
  Clarified "secondary remote" and added Codeberg as an example.

- "Why is the small research group messing around with raw DNS
  records?" Removed specific mention of DNS.

- "Describe pipelines using a workflow description language and
  register the pipeline with DockStore." Added.

- "Why is Signal being called out here?" Reframed Signal as an example
  of an out-of-band channel.

- "Small research groups rarely support a community ... up in arms at
  2 am."  Collaboration across timezones means that happens more than
  it used to :-) Softened the contact-tree framing.

- "Someone else has to break the service." Clarified that someone else
  breaks a test copy and the newest member executes the recovery.

- "Very few groups run something so critical that a 3 am outage
  ... uptime monitoring won't help with an active hacking attempt."
  Expanded the explanation of active hacking.

- "Explain why a registry listing that used to work might stop
  working." Added examples (token expired, account suspended, package
  removed or deprecated).

- "Web-based services should be monitored for up status, load status
  and response time." Added.

- "Explain how you can share credentials via the password managers
  ... isn't it also a security risk?" Added an explanation of shared
  vaults and the exposure trade-off.

- "Clarify that 'domain' is a 'DNS domain'." Changed to "custom web
  domain" as part of removing specific mention of DNS.

- "Advice about including contact information ... belongs in the
  disaster plan tip." Moved to Tip 2.

- "What is the basis for ... a single page with 11-point font?"
  Replaced with "keep the policy short enough that people will
  actually read and remember it."

- "Some of the advice ... is about complying with legal and ethical
  obligations." Added a note at the top of Tip 8.

- "Unplug ... only works when the machine is physically accessible."
  Added cloud-console and rack alternatives.

- "Shouldn't there be some discussion of physical security?" Added a
  brief mention, but I didn't want to expand the scope of the paper
  even further.

- Tips 9-12: "These sections seem good." Thank you.
