Review for "Twelve Quick Tips for Managing IT Disasters in Small Research
Software Teams" by Greg Wilson.

Thank you for the manuscript. Small research teams typically face various IT
issues over time and being both aware and aware of and prepared for larger
"disasters" is vital.  Therefore, I think this paper may serve as a valuable
source for a particular readership.

In its present form, the manuscript requires minor revisions of its contents
before it can be considered for publication. Please find my comments and remarks
below.

General comments

My main concern is the scope of the manuscript and the actionability of several
tips for its stated audience. Many research software teams, especially those
embedded within universities, may not have administrative control over IT
infrastructure, including account management, backups, and more, simply because
these responsibilities lie with institutional IT. In such settings, some of the
suggested tips cannot be implemented directly by the research software team. I
suspect that a substantial part of the journal's readership may be in a similar
position.

I suggest improving the separation between the actions that a research software
team can take independently from actions that typically require institutional
IT, and aspects that the team could verify or request when it lacks
administrative authority. This may improve the actionability of the tips.

Furthermore, the manuscript assumes that small research teams have the ability
to carry out a substantial set IT-related tasks (named above) and even
budgeting.  Completing such tasks alongside their respective primary research
duties may represent a significant workload and may require additional
expertise. Please consider identifying a minimal set of essential practices and
indicating which tasks could typically be delegated to specialists.

Tips 11 and 12 broaden the manuscript from technical disaster prep into
leadership. I acknowledge the relevance, but this seems out of the initial
scope.

Specific comments

- On the "Questions for Support" – what to do in case there is no such support
  available on site?

- Please consider adding web links into in the running text rather than having
  both footnotes and numbered references – in case the journal allows this.

- CRAN is a mirrored network, so there is no single failure point once a package
  is published (see "Who You Are")

- Data repositories do not primarily serve as platform for data backup, but for
  data publishing, hence the DOI. Research software groups probably want to back
  up raw data, and usually some form of curated data is published rather than
  raw data (Tip 3).

- The note field in Tip 3 seems redundant if the 3-2-1 rule is adhered to.

- Tip 6 seems to be a rule to monitor tip 1. Please consider whether aligning
  those would be sensible.

- In case o boarding is suggested, I think mentioning onboarding is similarly
  critical (Tip 7, box).

- Please consider refining Tip 8. This rule is not actionable at all for readers
  subordinate to local IT support.

- If a research software team is small with few members and just produces
  analysis code and some packages, the 12 tips seem disproportionate. Please
  consider adding a small acknowledgement of this.
